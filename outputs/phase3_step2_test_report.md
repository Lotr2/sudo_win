# Phase 3 · Step 2: Quality check report

**Result: 4 of 4 quality checks pass** (all of them), after 2 build rounds.

The product was built and checked in a sealed sandbox with no internet access (audit hook (sockets, DNS and subprocesses blocked)).

## Quality review

A reviewer scored the first version **3 out of 5** against a rubric (specific, practical, complete against the plan and the landing page, no filler). The builder revised it once to address:
- The guide does not include any ready‑to‑copy proposal template, so buyers cannot paste a finished document into their own email or document.
- There is no invoice template provided; the section lists required fields but buyers still need to create the layout themselves.
- The promise of design‑friendly layouts is not illustrated – the guide mentions clean fonts and spacing but offers no visual examples or guidance on how to format the proposal or invoice.
- The guide mentions pricing, scope, and payment terms but does not give specific tips on how to protect the designer’s time (e.g., setting clear boundaries or using time‑based pricing).
- The “copy‑paste a template” instruction is vague because no template is actually shown; buyers may be unsure what to copy.

After the revision the reviewer scored it **4 out of 5**. Still to improve:
- The guide does not give a clear, numbered step‑by‑step list for writing a proposal; buyers expect a simple sequence of actions.
- The proposal section does not explain how pricing, scope, and payment terms protect the freelancer’s time; a short paragraph or bullets would reinforce that promise.
- Design‑friendly layout tips for the proposal template are minimal; a visual example or a more detailed formatting checklist would help buyers create a professional look without extra software.
- The invoice template includes payment terms, but they are not highlighted as a separate “Payment Terms” section, making it harder for buyers to spot the key points that protect their time.
